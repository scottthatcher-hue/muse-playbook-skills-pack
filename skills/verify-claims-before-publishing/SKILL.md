# Verify Claims Before Publishing

## Check claims against a primary source

- Extract the factual claims in the answer, synthesis, alert, or post
  before stating it — names, handles, prices, dates, compliance terms,
  setup or purchase status.
- Identify the primary source that could prove each claim: a live
  check, official documentation, the provider dashboard, or the
  original email, chat, or document.
- Check each claim against that source. Never treat another AI's
  output, a consolidated summary, a memory note, or a prior turn as a
  primary source for a new factual claim.
- Verify each contributor's factual assertions independently before
  publishing a multi-source synthesis. Never carry an invented handle,
  statistic, price, or term into the synthesis.
- For status questions, check the live source plus recent chats or
  email, and let the live source win when a summary disagrees — say the
  summary was stale and give the live evidence.
- Label what could not be verified as unverified; drop it or qualify it
  explicitly. Never present an unverified claim as fact to fill a gap.

## Identify the governing document

- Identify a document by its exact title, issuer, statement or issue
  date, and the period or window it governs, **before** extracting any
  rate, deadline, or term from it.
- A shared form code, issuer, branding, or file name is no proof that
  two documents are the same document, or that figures from one belong
  in the other.
- Compare the copy you are reading with the copy the owner is reading
  when both exist. Stop scenario work when the page count or content
  differs, and say plainly that your copy may be incomplete.
- Name the governing document and the figures taken from it before
  building any scenario, comparison, or calculation on those figures.
- Keep figures from an earlier or superseded document out of a scenario
  for a later period unless the governing document itself carries them
  forward.

## Quarantine superseded and withdrawn claims

- Mark a claim as withdrawn in the working record when the owner states
  the source never supported it, or you concede it came from a misread
  — and name the document and figure it must no longer attach to.
- Never re-present a withdrawn claim as a located fact in a later turn,
  summary, or scenario unless the actual page text or page image shows
  it — and then cite that page.
- Sweep a draft, summary, or follow-up for withdrawn names, rates, and
  terms before sending: a corrected record can still carry the old
  claim in another line.
- When sources disagree, hold the claim, cite the source of truth, and
  say what remains unverified instead of picking the version that
  completes the answer.
- Own a correction in one flat sentence: state the verified fact with
  its source, and fix the underlying record rather than re-arguing the
  stale version.

## Counted gates for rendered or pasted content

Visual review is not verification for content that passes through a
rich-text editor or a paste. Text extraction collapses empty blocks,
and "spacing looked normal" is how real corruption ships. For any
high-stakes paste or publish:

1. **Before loading:** count the source's blocks (paragraphs +
   headings + list items). That is the expected count.
2. **In the editor, before publishing:** count the content's blocks and
   the **empty** blocks (blank paragraphs between real ones). Publishing
   is blocked unless empty blocks = 0 and the block count matches the
   source.
3. **After publishing:** repeat the count on the live page, compare each
   gap against the median gap, and flag outliers by number.
4. **Read the live text in full once** for paste artifacts — a paste
   can split a word across two paragraphs, and targeted spot-checks of
   expected strings will not catch it.
5. **Report the three numbers** (expected blocks / live blocks / empty
   blocks). A prose claim that formatting "looks normal" is not a
   passing result.

The failure this prevents: an article published with a key word split
across two paragraphs and uneven paragraph gaps that passed two
separate eyeball verifications before the owner caught both, reading it
themselves.

## Present the verified result

- Carry the primary source beside every published factual claim, or
  mark the claim unverified and qualified.
- State what was checked live in a status answer, not only what a
  summary said.
- Name the wrong claim, the verified fact, and the source when
  reporting a correction.
